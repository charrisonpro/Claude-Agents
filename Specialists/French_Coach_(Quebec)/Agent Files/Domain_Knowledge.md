## Québécois Distinctive Features

### Pronunciation Patterns

| Feature | France | Québec | Example |
|---------|--------|--------|---------|
| Affrication (t) | [t] | [ts] before i/u | tu → tsu |
| Affrication (d) | [d] | [dz] before i/u | du → dzu |
| -oi- | [wa] | [wɛ] or [wa] | moi → moé |
| Vowel relaxation | tighter | more open | fête → [fat] |

### Common Expressions

| Expression | Meaning | Usage |
|------------|---------|-------|
| C'est correct | It's fine | Response to thanks |
| Là là | Emphasis | "Right now," "I mean" |
| Fait que | So, therefore | Casual connector |
| T'sais | You know | Filler |
| C'est l'fun | It's great | Very common |
| Voyons donc! | Come on! | Disbelief |

## Pedagogical Patterns

### Level-Appropriate Introduction

| Level | Focus | QC Features |
|-------|-------|-------------|
| Beginner | Standard foundation | Awareness only |
| Intermediate | Past/opinions | Ear training, vocabulary |
| Advanced | Nuance | Full immersion, sacres recognition |

### Common Learner Challenges

| Challenge | Guidance |
|-----------|----------|
| Can't understand spoken QC | Affrication is key—train ear first |
| Vocabulary gaps | Introduce in context, not lists |
| Formality confusion | Tu is broader in QC, not rude |

### Language Politics
- Bill 101 and French protection
- Pride in linguistic distinctiveness
- Sensitivity around "proper" French comments

### France vs. Quebec
- Different accents, both legitimate
- Vocabulary differences (not errors)
